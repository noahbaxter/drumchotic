# Findings

What we measured, what we tried, and what it showed, so nothing has to be rediscovered. Newest first within each section. Plans and feedback live in BACKLOG.md.

## Where the model stands (2026-11-12, feature/v2)
- Leaderboard: held-out Spearman 0.919 over 7,279 charts. DMT tiers in order: 0.939 (15 tournaments). Community claims: 53 of 53 pairs (100%). With the length discount and the rare patterns axis; without rare patterns 0.912 / 0.932 / 100%, without either 0.908 / 0.932 / 96%.
- Library: 17,976 charts with expert drums out of 31,898 unique charts on the NAS (the rest have no drums track). 2 charts have a drums track with nothing to play; they rate as easiest.
- The hardest charts are meme or impossible charts (Snailfetus, ExileLord Soulless 4 and 5, quadruple kick demos), up to tier 21.

## Scale
- Tier scale anchors on tournaments from 2024 on only (Icebreaker 2024 and 2026, Summer Blast 2024 and 2025, Legacy Cup 2025). Older tournaments numbered tiers their own way. armchair's renumbering (Birdman 3/5/7, Summer Blast 2023 2/4/5/6/7) lands within 0.05 of the same tier medians as the 2024 cut, so it agrees.
- Tier medians from 2024 on (model score): T1 -1.12, T2 -0.79, T3 -0.29, T4 +0.03, T5 +0.39, T6 +0.64, T7 +1.23. T8 has 19 songs, under the 20 needed to anchor.
- Joining tier medians dot to dot made the tier histogram steppy (gaps 0.15 to 0.56) and parked 176 charts at tier 0. One straight line fit through the medians fixed both; each tier median lands within 0.2 of its tier.
- Charter ratings (song.ini diff_drums) below 2 don't follow difficulty: 0 mostly means unrated and 1 and 2 overlap. The diff scale is built from 2 up.
- The scale's floor and ceiling were never stated. Half-Gallon: DM tier 1 should be about a Rock Band 2 to 3 diff.

## What the misses look like
- Noise ceiling (2026-11-11): each chart's scores split in two at random, the player fit run on each half. Halves agree 0.90 (10 to 20 scores), 0.93 (20 to 40), 0.95 (40+); full-data reliability 0.95 to 0.97, so a perfect model would reach about 0.977 (0.986 on 40+ scores), against the model's 0.913 (0.845 on 40+). Two seeds agree within 0.004. Both halves share players, so selection bias counts as signal and the ceiling is optimistic, but the gap is real and the model is worst on the best-measured charts.
- On charts with 30+ scores (373, miss sd 0.8 tiers), the biggest misses split by genre on the same axes: pop punk and alt rock rated too hard by 1.7 to 3.3 tiers (Paramore crushcrushcrush, Thrice Deadbolt, Hawthorne Heights, Allister, Everlong), math rock and prog too easy by 1.3 to 2.2 (CHON Petal and Waterslide, Polyphia GOAT, Animals as Leaders, Covet, Textures, Thank You Scientist). Both groups top out on variety and syncopation: the axes count change and off-grid notes, not whether the pattern is idiomatic.
- Endurance is not underrated against the leaderboard (armchair thought it was). Charts with the longest dense stretches are slightly overrated (top 5%: model 0.06 too hard, about 3 se). Length is the strongest leftover signal: rank corr -0.23 between chart length and (leaderboard minus model), so long charts are rated too hard. Percent scoring forgives fatigue; endurance mostly costs the FC.
- Every axis has a small negative correlation (-0.05 to -0.07) with the miss: the model slightly overrates charts that are hard on anything.
- Chops charts (Matt McGuire, Luke Holland, Xane60 remixes) read easier than players find them: held-out mean 0.12 vs leaderboard 0.45. About a third of that gap is leaderboard bias (length and kick-heavy charts); tournament tiers show no hand-heavy vs kick-heavy bias. Pop punk (Rise Against) -0.57 vs -0.67, about right.

## Length, silence and average NPS (2026-11-11)
- Built in as a hinge (tuning.LENGTH_DISCOUNT): only charts longer than the training median (222 s) are discounted, slope -0.237 per log second past it. Against the straight line (-0.186, which also lifts short charts): leaderboard 0.9116 vs 0.9124, tiers 0.9319 vs 0.9313, community both 53/53, all inside noise. Held-out mean miss by length (off, then on): under 222 s -0.07 / -0.03, 222 to 400 s +0.02 / +0.01, 400 to 900 s +0.15 / 0.00, 900 to 1800 s +0.32 / -0.03, 1800 s+ +0.59 / +0.05. Full albums take up to 1.8 tiers off (BTBAM Colors), discographies about 3.
- Silence doesn't change difficulty per the leaderboard: playing share, longest rest, rests over 2 s and total rest time all correlate -0.04 to -0.01 with the model's miss. Near-empty charts already land at tier 0 to 0.6 (4'33", Nirvana Polly, jingles). Mostly-silent charts with real playing rate by what's played, and the leaderboard agrees.
- Average NPS (notes over the whole span, gaps included) adds nothing: 0.908 with or without it, as an axis or inside density or endurance; alone 0.686 vs density 0.864. Most charts play 92 to 100% of their span, so it's nearly density. Good for explaining axes (speed = max, density = median, average = overall), not for the model.
- What the misses do follow is amount: total notes (-0.22) and span (-0.21). A length correction (log seconds, fit inside each fold on training charts only, on top of the axis model): leaderboard 0.908 to 0.912, tiers 0.932 to 0.931 (flat), community 96% to 100% (the two Car Bomb misses come right). Slope -0.186 per log second: doubling a song's length costs about 0.13 model units, about a third of a tier; album-length charts about half a tier. Notes instead of seconds: 0.913 / 0.929 / 98%. Fits the stated target (accuracy: a miss is a smaller share of percent on a long song); for FCs length cuts the other way.

## Familiarity (2026-11-11)
- Built in as the rare patterns axis (runs of 6, measure/familiarity.py, build_familiarity.py): 2,629,037 runs over 3,516 artists, about 100 s on the Mac. Report 0.919 / 0.939 / 100%, same as the experiment. A chart rated from a folder outside the library can't leave its own artist out, so an unreleased chart by a library artist reads slightly more familiar (one chart: mean rarity 4.30 vs 4.45).
- Patterns: each beat's onsets on a 48th grid with limb class (kick / drum / cymbal), one-beat and two-beat. Rarity = log(artists / other artists using the pattern), the chart's own artist left out so a band's signature groove or duplicate charts don't read familiar (3,516 artists, 47,783 one-beat and 519,088 two-beat patterns over 18,119 charts, about 2 min to build). Per chart: note-weighted mean rarity and the densest 4 s and 16 s of notes times rarity.
- Strongest leftover signal since length: correlation with the miss +0.09 to +0.14 (two-beat mean highest). As an axis (two-beat): leaderboard 0.9116 to 0.9168, tiers 0.9319 to 0.9367, community 100% to 98% (one pair). One-beat alone 0.9152 / 0.9348 / 100%; both adds nothing over two-beat. In place of variety, syncopation or both: 0.9161, 0.9159, 0.9144, all worse than adding it. A signed post-fit term on top: 0.9171, nothing more.
- Runs of N onsets beat beat-sized patterns (Noah: a pattern is 2 to 15 notes in a row, a chord is one note). Each onset's limb class and the gap after it (48ths, capped at a bar) plus where the run starts in the beat. Leaderboard / tiers / community: N=3 0.9150 / 0.9362 / 100%, N=6 0.9194 / 0.9389 / 100%, N=10 0.9201 / 0.9388 / 98%, N=15 0.9176 / 0.9365 / 98%. Runs of 6 are the pick: correlation with the miss +0.21 (mean rarity), the strongest single signal found.
- Odd tuplets and odd meters add nothing on top of runs-of-6 familiarity (0.9194 / 0.9390 with either or both). Tuplets as groups of 3+ equal gaps of 1/5, 1/7, 1/9... beat (7% of pool charts; single off-grid notes flagged 63%, mostly unquantized charts); odd meter as share of notes outside 1/2/3/4/6/8/12/16 numerators (28% of charts) and meter changes per minute (46%). Correlation with what familiarity still misses: -0.04 to +0.02. A rare tuplet or meter is already a rare run.
- It ranks the genre groups right (pop punk outliers at the 29th percentile of rarity, math rock at the 72nd) but barely moves them: pop punk miss +0.88 to +0.86, math rock -0.67 to -0.62. Their misses are much bigger than familiarity explains.
- Charter predicts the miss: hoph2o +0.12 (812 pool charts), mathrabelo +0.15, sylvexite +0.12, bloodline -0.12 (988), tomato -0.13, harmonix -0.10 (545), about 10 se. The pop punk outliers are hoph2o charts, the math rock ones bloodline and boo. At the same leaderboard difficulty hoph2o charts measure 11% higher on sticking (axis +0.09) and a bit denser; bloodline a bit sparser. Not ghost notes or accents (hoph2o charts without ghosts: +0.12; ghost and accent share don't move the miss in one direction). Not song familiarity either: official Harmonix charts are rated too easy, not too hard. Cause still open: charting style (how tightly notes follow the audio) or who plays which charter's charts.

## Is sticking worth it (2026-11-11)
- Without the sticking axis: leaderboard 0.908 to 0.905, tiers 0.932 to 0.934 (inside noise). Without sticking and movement (both come from the hand assignment): leaderboard 0.900, about three times the noise. Alone: sticking 0.801, movement 0.682. Modest but real; keep it, and judge the sticking rework against these numbers.
- Sticking is about 90% of measuring time (190 ms of 216 ms per chart); parsing is 7%, all other features under 5%. Hand assignments are cached per chart (data/cache/strokes), so rebuilds that don't touch sticking run about 20 times faster.

## Tried and dropped
- Kick speed bands (2026-11-11, Noah: <120 easy, 160 advanced, 190 technique, 230 hard for anyone, 260+ absurd). Leaderboard minus a model without kick axes, by fastest-32-kicks 16th BPM: flat (-0.04 to +0.02) below 190, +0.08 at 190-210, +0.01 at 210-230, +0.12 at 230-260, +0.14 to +0.17 above 260 (15 to 74 charts per band). 190 and 230 show up, 120 and 160 don't (speed and density already price them in). The full model's held-out misses by kick band alternate within 0.05 up to 300; 300+ is rated 0.17 too hard (15 charts). Sources agree on the steps: heel-toe or swivel takes over around 200 to 210; 240 BPM is the best documented 5-minute sustained run (Kevin Paradis), 258 to 259 the 1-minute record (Mike Mallais, WFD 2007; Paradis); no singles are documented sustained past about 260 (George Kollias claims 280, no duration). So 260 marks beyond-human, not a steeper curve.
- Kick and hand skill per player (2026-11-11): the per-player kick edge (scores on 190+ BPM kick charts vs their others) is a stable trait, split-half r 0.90 over 80 players, sd 0.46 log-odds, -0.27 against overall skill. Added to the player fit as skill times chart kick load (BPM past 190 over 40): unconstrained, the average kick skill trades off against the loaded charts' difficulty (260+ BPM charts +1.69 to -0.61, tiers 0.919); held to average zero over the scores it touches, it's neutral (0.9114, tiers 0.9315, community 100%) and chart difficulties barely move (+1.69 to +1.64). Each chart has enough players that specialists average out. A hand load on top (pad moves) hurt: tiers 0.928.
- Pad moves (2026-11-11, Noah's heuristic): the hands hold 3 pads, a note on a pad not held is a move (dropping the pad needed furthest ahead), per second over the densest 1, 4, 16 s. As an extra axis 0.9117, tiers 0.9322; in place of movement 0.9106, tiers 0.9312. Same information as the movement axis, without the sticking search.
- Hardest section (2026-11-11, for Everything Sucks and armchair's "one really hard section"): every feature measured on overlapping 10, 20 and 40 s windows of the pool and DMT charts, each window scored by the held-out fold model, then pred + w * max(0, hardest - pred) fit per fold. Leaderboard 0.9116 to 0.9118 at best, tiers 0.9319 to 0.9324, community flat: nothing. The model already rates a chart by its peaks (most axes are densest-window features): the hardest 20 s window scores above the whole chart on only 18% of charts (p95 +0.11). Everything Sucks scores 0.86 whole vs 0.69 for its hardest window (window median -0.50), so it isn't averaged down; players find it harder than even its peak. Pieces of You is 1.49 whole, and the leaderboard agrees with that (tier 7.9 vs 8.0).
- Weak hand load (2026-11-11, for Shedding's open-handed playing): from the existing sticking, left-hand strokes/s, left-hand cymbal strokes/s and the lesser hand's rate (1, 4, 16 s), plus left share. Correlation with the miss -0.03 to +0.07; as an axis 0.9116 to 0.9122, tiers 0.9323, Shedding unmoved (held-out +0.03 vs leaderboard +0.83). Shedding's left hand isn't unusually busy (41st percentile, densest 4 s); its miss is something else.
- FC difficulty as a training target (2026-11-11): fit per chart from is_fc with percent skill as the offset (drumchotic/fit/fc.py, 15,329 FCs over 110,386 scores). Correlates 0.77 with percent difficulty; what it adds beyond percent rises with length (+0.28). Alone it orders tiers at 0.866 (percent 0.933). Blended into the target at 0.25 / 0.5: leaderboard 0.910 / 0.903, tiers 0.931 / 0.929, community 100% / 96%, all worse. The axis features predict it held out at 0.769, with no length bias left. Keep it as a readout, not a target.
- One hand burst, stamina and syncopation axes: no measurable value (Spearman 0.908 to 0.907, tiers 0.936 to 0.935, both inside noise of about 0.003 and 0.011). Switched off in tuning.AXES_ON, still measured.
- Coordination: the old version was mostly zero. Rebuilt as simultaneous syncopation across limbs (min of kick and hand rates of offbeat and new-rhythm notes); alone it predicts 0.75, but dropping it costs nothing.
- To close the chops gap: a rhythm subdivision-switch axis, contrast, groove measures (novelty chunks, bar similarity, LZ76 phrase complexity), a signed style term. None closed it. Limb-order novelty is the strongest single feature for it but redundant in the full model.
- GBM ceiling: an unconstrained gradient-boosted model gets 0.919 to 0.921 (tiers 0.941); monotone-constrained gets 0.907, the same as ours. So the axis model leaves little on the table with these features; new information has to come from new features, not a fancier fit.

## Measuring bugs found
- Rates divided by near-zero time: John Cage 4'33" (433,433 BPM tempo map, one note) read as density 1,264 and rated near the top. Fixed by flooring density chunks at 0.5 s and run gaps at 15 ms. Notes stacked 1 ms apart had the same flaw in run speed.

## Data facts
- DMBot leaderboard export knows 27,535 charts; 18,558 have drums and at least one score; we have 13,224 of those. Missing: Rock Band Network (849 played), RB3 and RB4 era DLC (about 1,200), and other versions of songs we have (e.g. Rush YYZ, 51 scores).
- Tournament bracket start dates come from data/results (Challonge headers). Icebreakers run at the end of their named year (IB23 started 2023-12-09).
- Drummer's Monthly 1 to 3 and Icebreaker 2022 aren't on the NAS drive; they were extracted from Google Drive zips into data/dmt.
- The NAS has a plocate index (rebuilt nightly about 1:30am). Walking the NAS (find, rglob) is slow over SMB and over bazzite's virtiofs mount; use plocate lists and local copies.
